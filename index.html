How SweepBook works

Properties are the permanent record: one per address, holding the flue type and size, the appliance, and access notes. Every visit is a job hung on that property, so next year's tech opens the property and sees the whole history — flue specs, creosote grade, findings, and what was quoted — before climbing the ladder.

Inspections use structured checklists following the scope of NFPA 211 Level I (readily accessible portions) and Level II (adds accessible attic/crawl/basement areas, video scanning of the flue interior, liner integrity, and clearances of concealed portions). Rate each item OK, Attention, Hazard, or N/A. Hazards and attention items print at the top of the customer report summary.

The due list counts a property as serviced by its most recent Sweep or Inspection job (repairs and estimate visits don't reset the annual clock). Annual due date = last service + 12 months.

Estimates are line items on a job — add the repair lines with prices and the total figures itself. Print the inspection report and the estimate goes out on the same page the customer signs.

Honest limits

SweepBook is a record-keeping and documentation tool. The checklists follow the general scope of NFPA 211 Levels I and II as published, but they are not a substitute for the standard itself, for CSIA training/certification, or for your own judgment on site. It does not take photos (note photo file names in Findings), does not schedule or send reminders (the due list is yours to work), and does not sync between devices — backup export/import is the transfer path.
